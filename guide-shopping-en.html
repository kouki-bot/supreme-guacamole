Shopping problems in Japan

If you bought a product in Japan, your options may depend on where and how you bought it, the product condition and the contract terms.

Returning an item bought in a physical store

For a change-of-mind return, Japanese stores are not generally required to accept returns by law. Check the store's posted return policy and keep your receipt. If the product is defective or different from what was promised, contact the seller promptly and explain the issue.

A product broke during the warranty period

Check the warranty certificate, purchase date, covered parts and exclusions. Keep the receipt and take photos or videos of the problem. Contact the seller or manufacturer through its official support channel before attempting repairs.

I signed a contract after a door-to-door visit or sales call

Some door-to-door and telephone solicitation contracts may qualify for a cooling-off period, commonly eight days from receipt of the legally required written documents. Exceptions apply. Check the contract documents and contact a consumer centre immediately if the deadline may be close.

Official support

  • Consumer Affairs Agency: Specified Commercial Transactions Act Guide
  • National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan
  • Consumer Hotline 188 information

Consumer Hotline 188 connects consumers to a local consumer affairs consultation service in Japan. Ask for language support if needed; availability may vary.

This page provides general information, not legal advice. Cooling-off eligibility depends on the type of transaction and other conditions. Confirm your case with a consumer affairs centre.
